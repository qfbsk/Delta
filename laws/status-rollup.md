29. **The window/ownership law set, decided 2026-11-08, laws 1–20 — full text in [project-window-ownership](window-ownership.md). This entry is a pointer, not a summary: read the law file, because every attempt to restate the set here has gone stale within the same day.** Headline, in the order the user gave it: **任务优先 — a live task pins its window, so while the task is in progress the window cannot be closed** (*"不不不现在是任务优先，任务在进行，窗口就不能关"*), which overrode both my "close kills the process" law and the framing debate; **every AI edit must have a window, detected continuously** (*"每时每刻，检测AI正在编辑什么，只要是AI编辑的，就一定有一个窗口"*); **事件必绑窗口** with **停止 living on the 事件调度器** (item 33); **AI output binds exactly one window** and the pin is scoped to the in-flight edit (item 34); **delta opens a window** for an edit that has none (item 35); **modes replace permission flags, and ownership follows "a human has had this window in the foreground"** (item 45 replaced the earlier "follows modification" wording that this item originally carried); **every operation by human or AI is automatically git and automatically the database**, settled in favour of **the DB being authoritative and git being generated**. My old corollary here — *"停止 is never refused"* — survives, but only after being relocated by law 8: the stop is a scheduler action, never folded into the close key.
30. **The default shell profile is delta's own** (*"你启动了一个shell。默认是我们的。就像wt启动pwsh"*), and the splits come from running programs inside panes (`nvim`), the way tmux does. This refines item 24 rather than reversing it: what was rejected was an in-app shell acting as the control plane's syntax and a sidebar typing into a resident panel; what is decided is **a real shell program that is simply the default profile**, which is consistent with "cmd and pwsh stay first-class profiles, never emulated or forwarded".
31. **Handover applies to terminal panes, not only editors** — the AI uses the terminal the way it uses an editor to test code, while the human watches it run; and the design goal is stated as three people working (human, an AI advised by a second AI who also advises the human). This is the case the per-pane `FollowWriter`/`Manual` scroll mode and the reveal overlay were already designed for.
32. **"这是 ai terminal，而不是 ade"** — the user's conclusion from the above, with the same sentence pair that killed the close-kills law. Treat ADE as a description we do not owe; the product category to satisfy is the terminal. The reason kite reads as IDE-shaped is that its layout reproduced VS Code's worldview while its code was entirely its own (verified: no VS Code anything in the repo; the only `xterm` hit is a key-encoding comment at [shell.rs:1744](file:///E:/Think/crates/kite-app/src/shell.rs#L1744)). Delta's terminal shape is to be derived from the control plane and the terminal-camp precedents, not by generalizing anything in kite.
33. **Two components own the window question, and the event↔window relation is total** (*"这是我们窗口管理器与事件调度器该探讨的事情"*, 2026-11-08): the **事件调度器** dispatches AIs and **anyone may stop an AI's task from it at any time**, after which *"随之，窗口还在，但是可以关闭了"*. Law: **事件必定绑定窗口，无窗口的事件不存在，与事件解耦的窗口才能被关闭** — so **stop lives on the scheduler surface and is never folded into the close key**. Plus **layers and window sleep**: tmux's window model for geometry and covering, an explicit z-order, and *what is underneath is not drawn* to cut load and performance cost, switchable back at any moment (*"类似于最小化窗口…相当于窗口睡眠"*). Verified support for the layer half: upstream [`tmux(1)`](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man1/tmux.1.html) has floating panes (`break-pane -W`, *"is lifted out of the tiled layout and made floating"*) and `move-pane` takes a **z-index** where *"zero is the front"* — so z-order over a tiled layout is terminal-camp machinery, no IDE-camp precedent needed.
34. **The pin is per current edit, and AI output binds exactly one window** (*"不不不，AI当前在编辑什么就什么不能关，但是AI编辑完的可以关"*, *"不是说20多个窗口帮一个任务，而是AI output绑一个窗口"*, 2026-11-08, correcting my inference that a whole task pins everything it touched). Two bindings, two lifetimes: output↔one window for as long as the event runs; in-flight edit↔the window showing it, released when that edit finishes — *"编辑完的可以关"* and it becomes an ordinary window. Consequence: **"必有窗口" is scoped to the instant of the write** (no invisible write, no standing obligation to keep it on screen), the pin count is the AI's *concurrent* edit count so window churn is a z-order matter not a hostage matter, and the un-terminating-task problem reduces to one output window with the scheduler's stop as the escape. Full text in [project-window-ownership](window-ownership.md) law 10.
35. **AI 编辑没有窗口的东西，delta 就开一个** (the law-10 fork was put to the user; answer *"开一个"*, 2026-11-08). Law 2 is read literally: a per-file window is created for the duration of that in-flight edit — the alternative (narrate the edit inside the AI's output window) is rejected. Law 3 applies first, so an existing window is **taken over rather than duplicated**: creation is idempotent per path. Three consequences I derived (mine, overturnable): **the forced window never takes focus** (看见 ≠ 焦点, the twin of 焦点≠所有权); **released windows need a place to go, and the place is sleep, not close** (sink to the bottom of the z-order undrawn — closing would discard the very evidence law 2 exists to keep); and **there are three window authors, not two** — 我开的 / AI 接管进来的 / 系统为事件开的, and the third is 可写 by construction because that is its whole purpose, which fills the default-permission gap for AI-originated windows I had flagged as needing his decision.

37. **Shell 归 AI 操控 exposes a non-reversible capability — my raise, and the *state* it proposed was superseded by item 40, while the *problem* it raised is still open.** (*"另外就是别忘了shell也可以归AI操控"*.) `可写` on an editor is reversible because of auto-git/auto-DB; `可执行` on a shell is **not reversible by anything we have** (`rm`, outbound network, installs sit outside every transaction). My proposal was three per-window states (可读 / 可写 / 可执行) — **that shape is dead**, the user replaced flags with the mode system. What survives: the observation, and the precedent that input routing is an explicit per-pane property rather than a focus side effect — herdr's `pane input --right-click herdr|pane` (verified). Note also that **input injection is the only action where we are necessarily in the path**, so shell handover gets trustworthy attribution and per-command records for free — the one surface where that is true.
38. **"没有 workspace 就管不了 AI 群组" — I think this premise is false, and herdr's own manual is the counter-evidence (my argument, adopted by nobody yet).** What the user names as the blocker: after rejecting the ADE positioning, herdr's project/workspace object can't be carried, but that object is what makes agents manageable there. Against it: herdr's manual states *"a pane exists whether or not it contains an agent"* and `agent start` requires an existing shell pane and **never creates, splits or moves layout** (both verified) — so herdr itself keeps agent semantics and topology as **separate layers**, and its agent management rides on **caller-context addressing** (`HERDR_WORKSPACE_ID/TAB_ID/PANE_ID`), not on the project metaphor. What a workspace actually buys us is (a) a root set and (b) an address. (a) is already ACP's: `session/new` takes `cwd` + `additionalDirectories`, and the session's effective root set is exactly that list. (b) is already the terminal camp's: **a named tmux session** (`new-session -s`), which our own "拓扑是持久数据" law already makes persistent — and it is precisely the handle that the killed-email design was missing (*"我用手机给邮件，他不知道我是指哪个工作区"* → it can know which **session**). The genuinely IDE-shaped parts of herdr to refuse are `worktree list|create|open|remove` and its sidebar (`sidebar_collapsed_mode` — she has one), i.e. the checkout manager and the panel, not the container. Reason the user's fear has a real mechanism: **scope follows the process in the terminal camp and follows the project in the IDE camp**; adopt the project as the organizer and file tree, permissions, git and addressing all migrate under it, and the terminal becomes a widget inside an IDE.
39. **How to make an agent conform without trusting prompts — analysis in [project-agent-compliance](../proposals/agent-compliance.md), adopted by nobody yet.** The user raised this as his main fear (*"问题是如何让AI理解我们…提示词…AI不一定按我们期望做的。比如他希望他用我们终端，他不知道语法，或者他就喜欢自己写脚本。这个管不住。我想OPENCODE也没有方案"*, 2026-11-08). Three separable problems with three different answers: **discoverability** (solvable, by machine-readable self-description — herdr `--skill` and `api schema --json`, Fresh `--skill`, MCP discovery, opencode custom tools, all verified to exist), **preference** (not solvable by instruction; only by cost — proposed criterion *"绕我们的成本必须高于走我们的成本"*), and **compliance** (not a language problem at all). My recommendation: regulate **location, not style** — *"他就喜欢自己写脚本"* is the terminal's own idiom, and his own scene says the AI uses the terminal like an editor, so the enforceable norm is **"每一次执行必须发生在窗口里"**, which law 10's output-window binding already provides. And the honest ceiling: **a tool-layer guard at any vendor only sees down to the layer it recognises, and a script is the universal currency for getting under that layer** — which is why opencode has no complete answer either, and why open item 1 (OS identity) acquires its first non-aesthetic justification: make the tree unwritable from outside so disobedience is **harmless** instead of illegal. The promise delta can keep is not "the agent will obey us" but **"nothing bad follows from it not obeying."**
40. **权限旗子被推翻，换成模式系统 + 改动即所有权** (2026-11-08, user's own revision: *"现在再次修改权限配置。不需要你为窗口设置可由AI接管"*). Modes: **`normal / insert / replace / agent`**, default `normal`, shortcuts in, **ESC out**, normal being the 快捷模式; `可执行` is confirmed to exist (*"当然有可执行"*) but rides on the mode system rather than a per-window flag. **Reads never require permission; takeover does** — and it is required **only if you modified the window**: untouched → AI takes it freely; you edited → your consent, and consent **auto-saves then enters `agent`**; AI-opened-then-left + you edited in non-share → you are editor **and owner**, so consent again. **`agent` mode's entry and exit are decided by the AI, never by the human.** **`share`** is the state the AI asks the human into: you may type, you may not close, you may not exit; the AI reads your keystrokes live, and telling it in the group switches it to `agent` at once. **Modes have two subjects** (from his own A-insert/B-agent example): input modes attach to the keyboard and ESC affects only the focused window; ownership modes attach to the window, survive focus changes, and are what governs 关闭 — so being in `normal` does not let you close a window that is in `agent`. **The group chat is a special window with no modes.** Full text in [project-window-ownership](window-ownership.md) laws 4 and 13–18. **Note: this item's ownership criterion — *"是否做出了改动"* — was itself replaced later the same day by "人类在前台打开过就要授权"; see item 45.** The rest of item 40 (mode list, agent entry/exit by the AI, `share`'s semantics, the two mode subjects, group chat being mode-less) stands unchanged.

43. **The finding that cuts against our own new law: tmux has no global ESC-exits-mode, by choice.** tmux reserves **only the prefix** and forwards everything else verbatim, so a pane's vim keeps its bindings without cooperation; and ESC means "leave" **only inside a mode that was entered explicitly** (copy-mode: `[` in, `Escape` out). Our law 13 says ESC exits modes globally, which is exactly the ambiguous case tmux declined to have — and `escape-time` exists as its admission that a lone ESC cannot be told apart from the first byte of an escape sequence without waiting. So delta's ESC law and adopted key law (item 6) are in a three-way collision with the terminal-camp precedent that adjudicates both.
