# 法则：窗口与所有权

> 本文是 Delta 设计记录的正式文本。同步自 Qoder 记忆条目 `project-window-ownership`（脚本 `tools/sync-from-memory.sh`，最近同步 2026-11-08）。


## The scenario that is the spec

The user's own scenario, told in his words on 2026-11-08: it's a terminal, you start a shell (**default is ours, the way Windows Terminal starts pwsh**), you type `nvim`, splits appear as in tmux; you select a pane and hand it to an AI by @-mentioning him in the group, he starts working; you open another `nvim` in another split and type, while watching the AI type next to you and seeing the highlighting; you summon a second AI that advises both you and the first AI — *"像三个人在工作"*; you compile with `gcc -o` and run; when the terminal itself is the question you hand **the terminal** to an AI, because *"AI使用终端像使用编辑器一样，来测试代码"*; and you conclude *"这是ai terminal，而不是ade"*. Everything below either comes from his sentences in this and the following turns, or is explicitly labelled as my derivation.

## The law set, current as of 2026-11-08

1. **任务优先 — the task is the primary object, and a live task pins its window: while a task is in progress its window cannot be closed.** (*"不不不现在是任务优先，任务在进行，窗口就不能关"*.) This **supersedes** the original law "closing a pane kills its process and revokes its lease" ([project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) item 5, reason *"关掉，不然你都不知道他还跑着"*). The old law's objection to lifecycle-coupling survives and is why the hook is **task→window**, not **window→process**: *"我们不能关闭窗口就停。因为AI写文件连窗口都不一定是我们所打开的"*. His own example still holds (*"我关上了nvim.那个写nvim的任务没停"*) — the window he closed was **his** view, not the task's window. Net effect: the visibility law (*nothing runs that is not visible*) is restored **hard and immediate**, enforced by refusing the close instead of killing the process.
2. **Every AI edit must have a window, and the pin lasts only while that edit is in flight.** (*"每时每刻，检测AI正在编辑什么，只要是AI编辑的，就一定有一个窗口"*, scoped by law 10's *"AI当前在编辑什么就什么不能关，但是AI编辑完的可以关"*.) Detection is continuous and is what **creates** a window for work we did not open; the pin holds only for the duration of the current edit. There is no latency gap and no "eventually visible" weakening. **Exception decided 2026-11-08: deletion is not covered — *"AI删除就删除呗，不显示"*** — a removal gets no window and shows nothing. Law 5 (自动GIT、自动数据库) is then the only record of it, which is exactly where **backup-before-delete stops being a policy and becomes the requirement** ([project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) item 5's safety net, and the reason "看不见" is survivable here but not for writes). Attribution is a separate problem — see derivations.
3. **If a window exists, the AI takes it over** — *"如果窗口存在，AI就接管"*, so **an AI can take over a human's window**.
4. **接管需要申请；申请的触发条件是"人类在前台看过这个窗口"，不是"人类改过它"。** (*2026-11-08 用户的第二次修改，替换了本条此前的"改动即所有权"判据*.) Stated as: **只要 AI 打算接管一个已存在的窗口，无论 shell 还是 editor，都要申请；一次就行；重点是最终归属是谁** — *"我们重点是看最终归属是谁"*. The exact rule is his own sentence: *"一旦此窗口由人类以非agent,share的形式在前台打开过，就需要重新授权"*, unpacked by his three cases:
   - **人创的窗口 → AI 接管要授权。** (*"人创了，AI要授权"*)
   - **AI 创、AI 改、AI 交还给人类，人类以前台 editor 模式看过 → 不管有没有改动，AI 再接管仍需授权。** (*"AI创了，AI改了，AI给你了，你前台看了（editor模式），不管该没改，得授权AI"*)
   - **人类没在前台打开过 → 不用授权。** (*"你没前台打开，不用授权"*)
   Consequences recorded with it: consent **auto-saves and enters `agent`** (*"你同意后，就自动保存，并进入AGENT模式"* — unchanged from the earlier version); reads still never require an application (*"读取不需要申请"*); and **this closes the hole I raised** — a window you only *looked at* (you ran `ps`, a build, a test, edited nothing) is now protected, because the trigger is having shown it in the foreground rather than having changed it. The per-window `AI可写`/`AI可读` flag remains struck (*"不需要你为窗口设置可由AI接管"*).
   Two things his sentence leaves loose, **filled in by me rather than asked back**, both overturnable:
   - **同意之后，这条"人类在前台看过"的记录就清掉。** Otherwise *"一次就行"* and "once it has been in the human foreground it must be re-authorised" contradict each other: every later takeover would ask again. So the question is asked at most once per human-foreground visit, and answering it resets the flag until the window is shown in the foreground in `normal`/`insert`/`replace` again.
   - **授权是按 (窗口, AI) 记的，不是只按窗口记。** *"重点是看最终归属是谁"* is about who ends up owning the window, so if AI-A holds a window under your consent, AI-B arriving later asks for itself. The alternative — consent shared by all AIs — is the less permissive reading of his own sentence about ownership.
5. **Every operation, human or AI, is automatically git and automatically the database** — *"无论AI，还是人类，操作都是自动GIT，自动数据库"*. This settles the direction: the **database is authoritative, git is a generated product**; nobody types `git commit` as the record of work.
6. **The terminal knows what the AIs are doing, including which files they created** — *"terminal能够知道AI都在干什么。所以AI创建了什么文件"*.
7. **Handover is not limited to editors.** A terminal pane is handed over the same way and the human watches the AI use it — the case the per-pane `FollowWriter`/`Manual` scroll mode and the reveal overlay were designed for.
8. **事件必绑窗口 — no windowless event exists, and only a decoupled window is closable.** (*"事件必定绑定窗口，无窗口的事件不存在，与事件解耦的窗口才能被关闭"*.) Owned jointly by the **窗口管理器** and the **事件调度器** (*"这是我们窗口管理器与事件调度器该探讨的事情"*). The scheduler dispatches AIs and **anyone may stop an AI's task there at any time** (*"人们可以随时利用任务调度器关闭AI的任务"*), after which *"随之，窗口还在，但是可以关闭了"*. So **停止 lives on the scheduler surface, never folded into the close gesture** — close never implies stop.
9. **Layers and window sleep; tmux's window model for geometry and covering** — *"我说了学习tmux的窗口"*. There is a **z-order**; what is underneath **is not drawn**, to cut load and performance cost (*"类似于最小化窗口，反正有个图层排序，下面的就先不画"*); and it can be switched back at any moment — *"相当于窗口睡眠"*.
10. **AI output binds exactly one window; a task does not bind everything it touched.** (*"不是说20多个窗口帮一个任务，而是AI output绑一个窗口"*) Two bindings, two lifetimes: **output stream ↔ one window** for as long as the event runs (this is where law 1's refusal applies), and **in-flight edit ↔ the window showing it**, released the moment that edit completes — *"编辑完的可以关"* means it stays and simply becomes an ordinary window. So **"AI 编辑必有窗口" is scoped to the instant of the write**: never an invisible write, no standing obligation to keep displaying it.
    **一个窗口可以同时绑多个事件 — decided 2026-11-08.** Asked whether to allow it, the answer was *"3肯定允许啊。如果不允许的话，那软件不就没法写了吗"* — refusing would make the software un-writable, so the question should have been answered by reasoning instead of put to him. **The thing being counted is the bound event (its output stream), never "the task"** — law 10's own subject is output, and a task is not the unit that pins anything. So: each event bound to the window adds one; each event that finishes subtracts one; the window becomes closable only at zero. Otherwise the first of two events to finish would unlock a window the second is still writing into.
11. **When the AI edits something with no window, delta opens one** — the fork left by law 10 was put to the user and answered *"开一个"*: law 2 is read **literally**, a per-file window for the duration of that in-flight edit; narrating the edit inside the output window is rejected. Law 3 applies first — an existing window is **taken over, not duplicated** — so creation is **idempotent per path**, and the new window is an ordinary editor view whose buffer the write lands into as a transaction.
12. **Window management is settled as a mode; its aesthetics come from tmux and ghostty, never kite** (*"美学学习tmux就行。之前kite完全别学。太丑了，学习ghosty，tmux的美学"*). Reference pool: [reference-ghostty](../references/ghostty.md) plus [reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md); see 记忆条目 feedback-no-more-kite-precedent，本目录未收录，规则见 ../AGENTS.md (the kite ban now covers appearance).
13. **Modes, not flags: `normal / insert / replace / agent`, default `normal`; shortcuts enter, ESC exits; `normal` is the 快捷模式.** `可执行` is confirmed real (*"当然有可执行"*) and is carried by the mode system rather than a per-window switch. **Inside an `agent` window on a terminal pane, 可执行 is blanket — the AI may do anything with the terminal** (*"我说过AI用终端可以做任何事"*, closing the per-command question; his scene already had it: *"AI使用终端像使用编辑器一样，来测试代码"*). **On the ESC-vs-0x1B collision his ruling is that law 20 already settled it** — *"键不是排除了nvim，不会在冲突了吗"* — i.e. because our own editor takes vim's *capability* and none of its *keys*, there is no second mode machine in our own windows to fight chrome's ESC. **What his sentence does not cover — foreign full-screen programs still run in place (law 19) and have their own ESC semantics — is answered in the derivation "Input modes are keyboard-routing states, which settles ESC" below rather than handed back.**
14. **`agent` mode is entered and left by the AI's decision, never the human's** — *"agent这个模式显然不能主动进入与退出"*, *"进入AGENT退出，都需要AI来决定"*.
15. **`share` is the state the AI asks the human into**: the AI needs the window temporarily, it becomes `share`, where **you may type but may not close and may not exit** (*"你可以输入，但是不能关闭，也不能退出"*). **The AI reads your input live** (*"agent实时看你的输入"*), and telling it in the group that you are done switches it to `agent` immediately (*"你在群组跟他说好了，他就会立刻切换为agent模式"*).
16. **Modes have two subjects, forced by his own example.** Different splits can hold different modes at the same instant, so modes are recorded **per window** (*"我们必须是状态按照窗口记录"*), *but* `normal` behaves like a whole-screen state: with A `insert` and B `agent`, ESC yields `normal` because focus left them, not because their modes changed; returning to B still finds `agent`; and **being in `normal` does not let you close B** (*"就算你是normal，你也关闭不了B窗口"*). Recorded conclusion: **input modes (`normal/insert/replace`) attach to the keyboard and switching affects only the focused window; ownership modes (`agent/share`) attach to the window, ignore focus, and are what governs 关闭.** tmux supports the split: its modal states are **per-client**, process state is per-pane ([reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md)).
17. **The group chat is a special window that has none of these states** — *"群组是个特殊窗口，没有这些状态"*. **It is not a side feature: the group is where the other laws get triggered** — handover happens by @-mentioning the AI there (*"你选择了这个pane。交给了AI，在群组中@他，他开始执行"*), `share` ends there (*"你在群组跟他说好了，他就会立刻切换为agent模式"*), questions and ballots go there (*"提问可以到群里，其他人可以给建议。投票机制"*, [project-group-decision-rules](group-decision-rules.md)), and it is the container itself (*"一个工作空间就是一个群"*). Its display rule is his too: *"群聊里应该展示的是任务与回复，但是思考过程不需要展示"* — the thinking stream appears only after you click in, in a new interface, and *"最好由ACP来统一展示"* rather than looking like each vendor's own CLI.
18. **In `agent` mode the human keeps sight and loses the keyboard** — *"不会影响，AI接管后，我们该看的还是看，至少不能编辑"*. So `share` and `agent` are deliberately **opposite** in what the human gets: share = 能输入、不能关、不能退; agent = 能看、不能编辑. This **closes the long-open "is takeover exclusive?" question**: exclusive, with the demotion on the keyboard rather than the screen. Consequence: reveal/`FollowWriter`/highlighting have a guaranteed audience in `agent` mode, so they are the human's only remaining channel there, not cosmetics.
19. **Full-screen programs launched inside a shell run in that pane — 原地开, tmux's way; the "new split" idea is dropped.** The user reversed his own earlier rule after hearing the tmux analysis: *"我突然发现你说的哪个tmux更好。这样能避免很多问题，就原地开就行"*. What survives from the original statement, still decided: **the keyboard must actually transfer** to the app in the pane (*"键盘没转移导致nvim没法自己用自己的东西了"*) and **the window survives the app's own exit** (*"nvim有自己的退出，你别推了，窗口还在"*). Note for the record: **tmux does not "prevent" vim — it delegates**, reserving only the prefix and forwarding everything else; and it has no global ESC-exits-mode by choice ([reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md)). **And this half of the law is ours, not tmux's**: verified 2026-11-08, tmux's default is the opposite — *"When the shell command completes, the window closes."*, with `remain-on-exit` as the opt-out and `respawn-pane` documented as *"Reactivate a pane in which the command has exited (see the remain-on-exit window option)"*. So *"窗口还在"* is **delta's chosen default**, and must never be cited as inherited from the teacher.
20. **自有编辑器：能力从 fresh / helix / nvim / vim / zed / vis 取，键位一个都不学；形态甚至接近 `cat`.** Given as the *reason* for that pre-existing decision, and the reason is exactly the vim/ESC problem: *"所以这就是我讨厌vim的理由…只取文本编辑以及编程能力不学键位，然后自做一个编辑器的原因。甚至有点类似cat。。"*. Consequence: **delta's chrome is the only mode system in the tree** — a window's mode is not a property of the app inside it but a record in one global machine. Naming hazard, verified: [helix is *"a post-modern **modal** text editor"* with "Vim-like modal editing"](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix), modes named **normal / insert / select** — the same words law 13 uses, so the inherited vocabulary will keep implying modality the editor must not have. Sharper form of the rule: **take helix's select-then-act algebra, not its mode machine and not its keys.**

## The surfaces and the names, decided 2026-11-08

He gave these after being told what was still open, and preface-checked me for asking again (*"然后你他妈还是有病是吧，我说了tmux"*) — the chrome arrangement is tmux's, and the remaining surface questions below are the answers, not new questions.

21. **调度器和每个 AI 的实时流式信息是同一个窗口。** *"调度器与每个AI的实时流式信息是一个窗口"* — and it is reached by clicking the AI: *"当然点击对应AI，有一个窗口，然后这个可以更好的关闭"*. So there is **one** window object serving both jobs: the dispatch/stop surface and the live stream (thinking included, per law 17's "点击后出现新界面"). Law 8's 事件调度器 is therefore **not a separate panel** — it is that per-AI window, which is why stopping is available there rather than folded into some other close key.
22. **群聊也是一个窗口。** Extends law 17 (*"群组是个特殊窗口，没有这些状态"*) with the two things it now has to do: **AI 任务可以在群聊里关** (*"我们也可以在群聊关闭AI任务"*) and **群聊关闭就是 AI 关闭** (*"群聊关闭，就是AI关闭"*). Consequences:
    - **Closing the group is a stop, not a bare close.** Law 8 forbids folding 停止 into the close gesture, so this is the **one window class where close *is* an explicit stop action** — legal because the group's close does not dissolve an event's output window (law 10: the group is not the window any event's output is bound to; the per-AI window is) and because stopping any AI's task by a human is permitted at any time. Derived, overturnable: **it must read as stopping its members** (a list of who stops, exactly like the refused-close list derived above), not as a silent destroy.
    - **"群聊关闭 = AI 关闭" is the second human-side exit from `agent` mode**, alongside the scheduler's stop (law 8 + my derivation that stop clears the mode). Law 14 is unaffected: the AI's own entry/exit stays its decision; the human here destroys the session container, which is a different act.
23. **界面上没有"工作区"这一层。** *"每个群聊开启后需要选择一个工作区，但是整个软件没有那个所谓的工作区"*. So a workspace survives only as **the root path a group chooses when it opens** — it is not a container in the object tree, not a panel, not a level you switch through. What this settles: **the sidebar contest is over** (there is no 工作区/项目 panel wanting the rail), and it lands on the argument I had raised against *"没有 workspace 就管不了 AI 群组"* ([project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) 38): an agent's scope root is exactly ACP's `session/new` `cwd` + `additionalDirectories`, and its address is the group/AI/session, not a project metaphor. Derived, overturnable: **the group→workspace binding is chosen at open and is what gives every AI in that group its jail root and its file-tree root**; two groups may pick the same directory, and then they are two scopes over one tree, which is a ledger question for the later phase, not a chrome question.
24. **Names: 自有编辑器 = `miku`，shell = `solid`** (*"编辑器的话叫miku。shell的话叫solid"*). These are product names, so they are what the binary, the pane title and the CLI verbs should use.
25. **solid 的语法接近 bash 但不是同一门语言；只有基本内建；`$` `@` 这类符号不给 shell 用。** (*"最好是bush类似语法但是不太一样。我们只有基本cp md cd ls pwd 等等，然后没有那些$ @ 等等的 因为这些我们以后要充当自己的语法符号"*). Note `md` is cmd.exe's directory-creation builtin sitting in the same list as bash's `ls`/`pwd` — so "bash-like" means *shape*, not *dialect*. And the reservation is not cosmetic: **`@` is already delta's communication primitive** ([project-delta](delta-reasoning.md): 群组里 @ 他他开始执行), so a shell that consumed `@` or `$` would collide with delta's own syntax inside the one channel where every persistent fact is supposed to be reachable by typing. Law 25 is where that collision gets prevented. Still his to define later: the rest of the builtin set ("等等"), and what `$`/`@` finally mean in delta's syntax.

## Sourced reference — tmux (verified 2026-11-08)

Full text in [reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md). Load-bearing quotes used above:

- **Floating panes with real stacking order are upstream**: `break-pane -W` — the pane *"is lifted out of the tiled layout and made floating"*; `move-pane` takes a **z-index** where *"zero is the front"* ([`tmux(1)`](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man1/tmux.1.html)). So law 9's ordering is terminal-camp machinery; **the "don't draw what's underneath" half is ours, not tmux's** (unverified there).
- **`escape-time`**: *"the time in milliseconds for which tmux waits after an escape is input to determine if it is part of a function or meta escape sequence"* — tmux cannot tell a lone ESC apart without waiting, and the vim community tunes it down to 0/10 ([tmux#3843](https://github.com/tmux/tmux/issues/3843)).
- **Background windows stay live**: *"Each session is displayed on screen by a client"*, non-current window output is not shown until you switch, and `monitor-activity` flags windows with activity — the precedent for occluded ≠ suspended.

## Derivations (mine, not his words — overturnable)

- **Visibility is intercepted; attribution is not.** Laws 1+2 guarantee visibility structurally, with no latency to race. Attribution still relies on *observing* rather than *being in the path*: an AI's own tool, a build script and a program it launched look alike from the file system, and ACP never obliges a capable agent to route writes through us. What can make the actor reliable is metadata the operation path leaves (per-actor OS identity, or the DB row written at the moment of action) — see [project-agent-compliance](../proposals/agent-compliance.md).
- **Process ownership must live in the server.** Closing must not kill, detaching must not kill, and a pane that *cannot* be closed may still sit far from the process feeding it — so the PTY belongs to the session object, not the view. tmux and herdr both do this (herdr keeps the process on the session and only `cwd` on the pane).
- ~~**The invariant needs a deletion case.**~~ → **Answered 2026-11-08: it has none. *"AI删除就删除呗，不显示"*** — a removal is not an "edit that must have a window", so no window is opened and nothing is displayed. Recorded with it as the consequence: the only surviving trace of a delete is law 5's automatic git + database row, which is what makes backup-before-delete load-bearing instead of merely cautious, and what makes the delete case the **one** place where the visibility law is deliberately waived. (My earlier worry — "the pinned window would show nothing, so the law is unsatisfiable" — is resolved by declaring the law inapplicable rather than by inventing a removal view.)
- **"Agent splits panes, works, then cleans up" is closed by refusal, not bookkeeping.** It cannot dismiss the window its running task is pinned to, so evidence cannot be tidied mid-task; after the task ends, unpinning is legitimate cleanup.
- **窗口睡眠是"不能关"的出路。** A window that cannot be closed can still be pushed down the z-order and undrawn, so pinned windows are survivable: the pressure is bookkeeping, not screen. This retires the chrome item I had ranked above the sidebar contest — **overflow *is* the layer stack**, not a tab bar.
- **Occluded must not mean suspended.** A sleeping window still holds a live process and its pin, and the scheduler keeps dispatching into it. **But the performance argument only covers drawing**: the retained grid/scrollback of a chatty AI keeps growing whether or not it renders, so sleep needs its own bound — one cap should serve both this and law 11's churn.
- **The forced window must not take focus.** It exists to show a landing edit; stealing focus would let the AI interrupt the human's typing, which his own scene forbids (*"你开始打字。看到旁边分屏AI在打字"*) — **看见 ≠ 焦点**, the display-side twin of 焦点 ≠ 所有权. Window manager's job, not the scheduler's.
- **法则 11 开出来的窗口要有个去处，去处就是睡眠。** Nobody closes the windows delta opened on the AI's behalf, yet closing them would discard the evidence law 2 exists to preserve. So: **released ⇒ sunk to the bottom of the z-order and undrawn**, reachable, cheap, not deleted.
- **There are three window authors, not two.** 我开的 / AI 接管进来的 / 系统为事件开的. Only the first two are permission questions; the third is a consequence — it exists precisely to receive that event's write. This fills the default-permission gap for AI-originated windows that I flagged earlier and was told was a needless question.
- **停止 must clear `agent` mode.** Entry and exit are the AI's alone (law 14); the only human-side exit is the scheduler's stop (law 8). If stop merely ends the task row, a hung agent yields a window nobody can close **and** nobody can leave. A safety property, not a convenience.
- **Auto-save at consent is what makes handover attributable.** The agreed snapshot is a boundary row: the human's edits end there, the AI's begin there, and git/DB get a clean cut instead of an interleaved buffer.
- ~~**The new permission rule is more permissive, and has one hole.**~~ → **Closed by the user on 2026-11-08 while answering it.** I had argued that keying consent to *modification* left "误杀优于静默" backwards — a window you only observed (ran `ps`, a build, a test) was freely takeable and nothing in the ledger recorded the loss — and proposed counting "input happened / a live process is attached" as 改动. He replaced the criterion instead of patching it: **the trigger is a human having had the window in the foreground in a non-`agent`, non-`share` state** (law 4), which is strictly broader than modification and therefore also covers pure observation. My proposed "count input as 改动" is **withdrawn as unnecessary**, and with it the whole permissiveness objection.
- **"人类在前台看过就要授权"这条规则换来一个新字段，但比改动版省事。** No 可写 toggle needs a key, but the window record must carry `owner` plus **"has a human had this window in the foreground, in which modes"** — that is what decides whether a takeover asks. It is history, not a boolean, so it stays a ledger query and 焦点 ≠ 所有权 keeps getting used constantly. And it is **cheaper than the "did the human change anything" version I replaced**: having-shown-it-in-the-foreground is something we already know for other reasons (focus, drawing), whereas "did the human change anything" would need diffing a buffer we may not own (a foreign app's grid).
- **Input injection is the one place we are necessarily in the path.** File writes can bypass us; keystrokes an AI sends into a pane arrive through delta by construction. So shell handover gets reliable attribution and per-command records for free.
- **Two permission surfaces remain distinct.** Reading a path is not reading a window: a window holds an unsaved buffer, cursor, and scrollback; a path holds disk bytes. Under law 4's replacement this matters less (reads never ask), but the editor-vs-foreign-app split reproduces it: our own buffers are exact, foreign grids are inferred.
- **Ghostty's vocabulary is available and is not our rule.** Ghostty fades unfocused splits (`unfocused-split-opacity`, *"slightly faded out"*) but still draws them; our sleep law says occluded ⇒ not drawn. Compatible: fade is the *appearance* of sitting low, the cull is its *cost*. And `minimum-contrast` is a principle, not a knob: any translucent background must enforce a contrast floor on what is drawn over it, AI highlight overlays included.

## Derived answers (mine, decider = Qoder on the standing instruction to work things out; every one is overturnable)

Written 2026-11-08 after the user refused to be handed these back (*"chrome我不是说了学tmux…你他妈每次都让我给你设计"*, and before that *"你用脑子想想在问我行不"*). Each answer below cites the law or the verified precedent it is derived from, so overturning one only requires disagreeing with that source.

- **Input modes are keyboard-routing states, which settles ESC and `replace` together.** Read law 16 literally — input modes attach to the **keyboard**, ownership modes to the **window** — then `normal / insert / replace` describe *who receives keystrokes at delta's layer*: `normal` is the 快捷模式 (law 13: chords live, delta holds the keyboard), `insert` hands them verbatim to the pane, `replace` is `insert` with overtype. Three consequences, all forced:
  - **`replace` is a chrome mode, and law 20 does not conflict with it.** Overtype is a *typing behaviour*, not a vim keybinding, so "keys from nobody" is untouched; and being an input mode it never gates 关闭 and never enters the ownership record (law 16). What law 20 forbids is a second mode *machine* inside our editor, and `replace` is not one — it is one value of delta's single machine.
  - **ESC for a foreign full-screen app is not a collision, it is already decided by the definition.** In `insert` the keys go to the pane, so ESC reaches the app by construction; delta cannot also interpret it. So chrome's mode exit in that state is the **prefix**, exactly tmux's model — tmux reserves *only* the prefix and makes ESC mean "leave" *only* inside a mode entered explicitly (`[` in, `Escape` out) ([reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md)). Law 13's ESC-exits rule therefore applies wherever delta owns the keymap: our own editor (law 20 makes ESC unambiguous there, which is the user's own ruling: *"键不是排除了nvim，不会在冲突了吗"*) and any pane in `normal`.
  - **Do not copy tmux's `escape-time`.** It exists because tmux tries to interpret an ESC it received (*"the time… for which tmux waits after an escape is input to determine if it is part of a function or meta escape sequence"*). Delta never waits on a bare ESC: either the pane owns the byte (instant) or delta owns the keymap and synthesised sequences are distinguishable without a timer. This is also the answer to the "is delta's global ESC law in a three-way collision with herdr keys" worry — the collision only exists if chrome attempts to read ESC out of a stream it has already handed over.
- **The status bar's `insert` and a foreign app's "INSERT" are two different facts, so there is nothing to reconcile.** Ours reports delta's keyboard-routing state for the focused pane; the app's word is that app's grid content. Under the chrome rule *one authoritative location per fact* ([project-delta](delta-reasoning.md)), the split is by subject, not by priority. Cosmetic similarity is a styling matter for the user, not a semantic open item.
- **The pin counts live *event rows*, not inferred agent state.** Law 10 binds the window to an event's output, so the unit is "an event row is open against this window" — its process has not exited — and never `done`, which is an inference or a self-report and, per herdr's verified split (`report-agent --state` accepts only `idle|working|blocked|unknown` while `done` is watched; [reference-herdr](../references/herdr.md)), must not share a slot with observed state. Consequences: a shell parked in `less` still pins (its child is alive); a shell back at a prompt with no open event row closes freely; and **open item 2 (may `done` be self-reported?) no longer touches window closing at all** — it survives only for the status display and the ballot roster. Precedent for the check being a process query, not a guess: herdr's `pane process-info` and `pane wait-output --match/--regex --timeout` ([reference-herdr](../references/herdr.md)).
- **No cap is needed on how many law-11 windows are drawn at once, because the machine's windows never enter the human's layout.** Law 9 says what is underneath is not drawn, so "drawn at once" is 1 by construction and the question that remains is *placement*. Both adopted precedents answer it: herdr's custom commands have `type = "popup"` — *"opens a session-modal terminal without changing the tab layout"* ([reference-herdr](../references/herdr.md)) — and tmux's floating panes are explicitly *"lifted out of the tiled layout"* with their own z-index ([reference-tmux](../references/tmux.md)). So: **事件为 AI 的写入开出来的窗口进图层栈，不进人类的分屏行**; the human's split count never grows because of the AI, and law 2's evidence requirement is met by the rows existing and being counted, which is the same "exists-but-invisible must be counted" invariant the chrome layer already carries.
- **A refused close must name the bound events and land on the scheduler row that can release them.** Derivation, not placement: law 1 makes the only escape from a pin 停止, and law 8 puts 停止 on the 事件调度器 and forbids folding it into the close gesture. So a refusal that says only "不能关" is a dead end that violates law 8's separation — the refusal is a *list* (law 10: one row per bound event, since a window may carry several) plus the jump to each event's scheduler entry. Where that UI goes and what it looks like is the user's; **which object it jumps to is forced**.
- **`share` needs no timeout; it reuses the parked shape already agreed for ballots.** Law 15's state is *waiting on a human*, which is exactly what the delegated reading of *"写lease，是两种"* already defines — a parked holder keeps its pane and identity, loses write authority, and **displays who waits and for how long** ([project-group-decision-rules](group-decision-rules.md), [project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) 1.20b). Its two exits are the ones already written: 在群里说好了 → `agent` (law 15), and 调度器停止 → unpin + clear the ownership mode (law 8 plus the derivation above that stop must clear `agent`). Law 14 is not offended: the AI's own entry/exit remains its decision, and here the human dissolves the *event*, not the mode.
- **An AI that never votes cannot wedge a ballot, and it needed no new rule.** Rule 13 closes the window by exhaustion with 投完 = every invitee voted and no skipping, so the roster is the exposure; but stopping an AI is available to anyone at any time on the scheduler (law 8), and **only the human may initiate a vote, and ballots may never authorize anything** ([project-group-decision-rules](group-decision-rules.md), [project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) 1.20a) — so an unresponsive voter can neither start nor grant anything. Derived rule: **the roster is computed from invited-and-live actors**, so a stopped AI leaves it and the ballot closes. No timeout on the ballot itself.
- **"每一次执行必须发生在窗口里" needs no new law and no new machinery — and its enforceable content is narrower than the sentence.** Law 8 (无窗口的事件不存在) plus law 10 (one output window per event) already make every execution delta *knows about* land in a window. What the sentence adds is about executions delta is *not* told about, and that half is exactly the ceiling [project-agent-compliance](../proposals/agent-compliance.md) records: a tool-layer guard sees a tool call, not what a launched script did. So the law is adopted in the form **"delta 只承认窗口里的执行；不在窗口里的执行不受我们保护，也不由我们记账"**, whose real teeth are the deferred OS-identity boundary, not a prompt.
- **The path-rule vs window-consent collision cannot occur this phase, so it is parked rather than unanswered.** The user's ruling is that permissions ship as a blank slot (*"数据库，git，备份权限管理，留好空白。以后再做…现在权限先防空"*, [project-delta-status](status-rollup.md) 52). With no path rules in the first build, case (b) (path says 禁止, window says 授权) has nothing to fire, and case (a) (path allows, law 4 asks) is decided by law 4 alone: **consent is required regardless of any rule that may later allow the write**. My ordering proposal — 路径规则是上限，窗口授权只是开门 — stays recorded for the phase that builds the engine so it can be adopted or refused with an engine in front of it.
- **Foreign apps in our windows: display is best-effort, authority never is.** The tolerance limit for "别人家的程序在我们自己的窗口里，看得没有我们自己的编辑器清楚" is drawn at the attribution line, not the pixel line: **anything that grants, records or pins takes its evidence from input injection and from our own buffers, never from a foreign grid** (the derived fact that keystrokes must come through delta, and ACP's inability to oblige a client to route writes through us, both point here). A grid-inferred read is allowed to be wrong and is therefore allowed to be *displayed* as uncertain; it is never allowed to be the basis of a decision.
